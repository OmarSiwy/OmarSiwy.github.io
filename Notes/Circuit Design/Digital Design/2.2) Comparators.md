**Inputs.** Two $w$-bit unsigned integers $X$, $Y$ (fixed-point operands reduce to this at no cost, see below).
**Outputs.** Up to three mutually exclusive Booleans $X<Y$, $X=Y$, $X>Y$ — *only the ones the context asks for*; building all three when you need one is the most common waste in this block.
**Accuracy.** Exact, $0$ ulp. A comparator does no arithmetic on values, only on order, so there is nothing to round and no error budget to spend (contrast [[1.2) Error Accuracy]]).
**Optimized for.** LUT/gate count first, logic depth second. Comparison is almost never the critical path on its own — it is a leaf inside floating-point compare ([[2.7) Floating-Point Operators]]) or an exponent difference inside FP addition, so its area is what multiplies up.

## Fixed-point considerations

Fixed-point comparison reduces to unsigned integer comparison **at zero hardware cost**, which is why the rest of the chapter only talks about integers.

- **Same format.** Two numbers in $\mathrm{ufix}(m,\ell)$ compare exactly as the integers $2^{\ell}X$ and $2^{\ell}Y$: multiplying both sides of an inequality by the positive constant $2^{\ell}$ preserves order. The position of the binary point is irrelevant — it is not a signal, it is a label ([[1.1) Number Formats]]).
- **Different formats.** $\mathrm{ufix}(m_X,\ell_X)$ and $\mathrm{ufix}(m_Y,\ell_Y)$ are first converted to the smallest common format $\mathrm{ufix}(m,\ell)$ with $m=\max(m_X,m_Y)$ and $\ell=\min(\ell_X,\ell_Y)$. This is pure zero-padding: it changes neither value, hence neither the comparison result. The padding is not wasted, though — a padded operand carries **constant zero bits**, and constant bits are exactly what the constant-comparison optimization below feeds on.
- **Two's complement.** The MSB carries the same magnitude weight as in unsigned, with a negative sign. Two signed operands of the same format therefore compare like the unsigned numbers obtained by **exchanging their MSBs**. If the MSBs are equal the exchange is a no-op; if they differ it is the same as complementing both, i.e. adding the bias $2^{w-1}$ to both operands — order-preserving either way. A wire crossing, no logic.

## Area and delay considerations

Two baselines, both from first principles.

**Equality.** $X=Y$ iff every bit agrees:

$$X=Y \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \bigwedge_{i=0}^{w-1}\overline{x_i\oplus y_i}$$

$w$ XNOR gates, all in parallel, then an AND tree. AND is associative, so the tree can be balanced: depth $\lceil\log_2 w\rceil$.

**Ordering.** $X<Y$ is the sign bit of $X-Y$, and equivalently the borrow out of the subtracter. Working the other way round with the two's-complement identity $-Y=\overline{Y}+1$:

$$X + \overline{Y} + 1 \;=\; X + (2^w-1-Y) + 1 \;=\; 2^w + (X-Y)$$

so the **carry out** $c_w$ of $X+\overline{Y}+1$ is $1$ exactly when $X-Y\ge 0$, i.e. $c_w = (X\ge Y)$ and $\overline{c_w}=(X<Y)$. The other three predicates are free: negate for $X\ge Y$, swap the operands for $X>Y$ and $X\le Y$.

That single bit is the *leftmost* output of a carry chain, so it can be had in $O(\log w)$ using only the leftmost path of any prefix tree from [[2.1) Adders]] — none of the sum bits, and none of the intermediate carries, need to be built. Discarding them is the whole "computing just right" saving here; the asymptotic complexity is unchanged.

## The basic binary tree comparator

Split at $k=\lceil w/2\rceil$: $X=2^kX_H+X_L$ and $Y=2^kY_H+Y_L$. Then

- if $X_H = Y_H$, the order of $X$ and $Y$ is the order of $X_L$ and $Y_L$;
- if $X_H \ne Y_H$, the low halves are irrelevant.

Encode each node's verdict as the pair $(G,E)$ with $G=[X>Y]$ and $E=[X=Y]$. Reading Table 6.1 (p. 147) column by column gives the recurrence

$$\boxed{\;G_{H:L}=G_H \vee (E_H \wedge G_L),\qquad E_{H:L}=E_H \wedge E_L\;}$$

At the leaves, single bits, with no logic needed if $X>Y$ is encoded $10$, $X<Y$ is encoded $01$ and $X=Y$ is either $00$ or $11$:

$$G_i = x_i \wedge \overline{y_i},\qquad E_i = \overline{x_i\oplus y_i}$$

Recursing gives a binary tree of depth $\lceil\log_2 w\rceil$ with two bits on every edge.

> [!note] The comparator *is* the carry chain, not merely like it
> The adder's carry recurrence over a block is $g_{H:L}=g_H\vee(p_H\wedge g_L)$, $p_{H:L}=p_H\wedge p_L$ — the same two lines with $(G,E)$ renamed $(g,p)$. This is not an analogy. Instantiate the adder on the subtraction $X+\overline{Y}+1$ and its per-bit signals come out as
> $$g_i = x_i\wedge\overline{y_i} = [\,x_i>y_i\,],\qquad p_i = x_i\oplus\overline{y_i} = \overline{x_i\oplus y_i} = [\,x_i=y_i\,]$$
> literally $G_i$ and $E_i$. **Generate is "this chunk decides, and it says greater"; propagate is "this chunk abstains".** Since $\circ$ is associative — same proof as in [[2.1) Adders]], do not re-derive it — comparison is an associative prefix reduction and every prefix topology transfers unchanged: [[Kogge-Stone Tree]] for depth, [[Brent-Kung Tree]] for area, the FPGA carry chain for both. That structural identity is why a comparator can be built out of an adder's carry logic instead of dedicated hardware, and it is the reason every FPGA construction below works.

**When the tree wins.** If only $X=Y$ is wanted, the recurrence collapses to $E_{H:L}=E_H\wedge E_L$ alone: one bit per edge, and the tree degenerates into the AND-of-XNORs above. If only $X<Y$ is wanted there is **no** such simplification — every node except the root must still distinguish three cases, so it still carries two bits. Asking for less does not always buy less.

## FPGA-specific implementations

### Exploiting the fast carry logic

The naive route — instantiate a $w$-bit subtracter, keep the carry out — costs $w$ LUTs on the carry chain. It can be halved (p. 148, after [PZC10]) by exploiting the basic logic element structure of Sect. 5.4 (p. 125): a modern BLE computes $\mathrm{FA}(p(a,b,c,d),\,q(a,b,c,d),\,c_i)$, i.e. **two** arbitrary 4-input functions feeding one hard full adder ([[Lookup-Tables]]).

Feed $(a,b,c,d)$ with two *aligned 2-bit chunks* $X_i$ and $Y_i$. The two LUTs emit one bit each, $x'_i$ and $y'_i$, that compare the same way $X_i$ and $Y_i$ do (Table 6.2, p. 148):

| | $X_i<Y_i$ | $X_i=Y_i$ | $X_i>Y_i$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x'_i$ | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| $y'_i$ | 1 | 0 | 0 |

A subtracter on the fast carry logic then computes $X'-Y'$, whose sign is that of $X-Y$. Tracing $x'_i+\overline{y'_i}+c_i$ against the encoding shows the chain does exactly the prefix reduction of the previous section:

| chunk verdict | $x'_i$ | $\overline{y'_i}$ | $c_{i+1}$ | prefix role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $X_i>Y_i$ | 1 | 1 | $1$ | generate ($G_i$) |
| $X_i=Y_i$ | 0 | 1 | $c_i$ | propagate ($E_i$) |
| $X_i<Y_i$ | 0 | 0 | $0$ | kill |

Cost: $\lceil w/2\rceil$ LUTs for $X<Y$ — the most area-efficient solution when only that bit is needed, and the default synthesis tools already emit.

For $X=Y$, chunk differently: a wide AND of per-chunk equality bits. A LUT6 takes 3 bits of $X$ and 3 of $Y$, so on recent AMD devices each LUT6 handles a 3-bit chunk, total $\lceil w/3\rceil$ LUTs. Both of these are also the best *latency* for widths up to 64 bits (p. 148) — below that, a chain that is one carry-propagate deep beats any tree, because the FPGA's dedicated carry path is far faster than the general routing a tree's edges must cross.

### The two-level tree of fast carry logic

Past a few hundred bits the $w/2$-long carry propagation stops being cheap. Split $X$ and $Y$ into chunks of $k$ bits and compare each chunk **in parallel**; encode every chunk verdict in the two bits of Table 6.2, which assembles the $k'$ chunk results into two synthetic $k'$-bit integers $X'$ and $Y'$; compare those with a second fast carry comparison. This is legitimate precisely because the encoding is the $(G,E)$ pair and the second level is the same $\circ$ operator applied one level up.

With $kk'\ge w$ the delay is balanced at $k\approx k'\approx\sqrt{w}$ (p. 148). In a pipelined design $k$ or $k'$ is instead chosen to fill the delay left in the current pipeline stage — this is what [[FloPoCo]] does when a comparator sits inside a larger pipeline.

Cost (p. 149): the second level is small, about $\sqrt{w}/2$ LUTs, but the first level must compute the chunk equality bits, about $w/3$ LUTs — so the total is well above the plain $w/2$. Generalizations exist: a LUT6 supports a ternary comparison tree instead of the binary tree above, and fast carry logic supports a $p$-level tree of $\sqrt[p]{w}$-bit comparators, mixable and matchable. For practical sizes none of them beat plain fast carry on area; the two-level tree earns its keep only at very low latency for sizes of several thousand bits.

### Comparing against a constant

Freeze $Y=C$. In the tree formulation the synthesis optimizer simply deletes the parts of the tree that became constant, and the per-bit leaves collapse completely: substituting the known $c_i$ into the leaf equations,

$$
c_i=0:\quad G_i = x_i\wedge\overline{0}=x_i,\;\; E_i=\overline{x_i}\qquad
c_i=1:\quad G_i = x_i\wedge\overline{1}=0,\;\; E_i=x_i
$$

by *complement of a constant* and *absorption*. Every XNOR and every AND against a constant becomes a wire or an inverter. Nothing is left at the leaves — the only surviving logic is the $\circ$ reduction over the chunk.

The real win is therefore in **LUT packing**, not gate count. A LUT-$\alpha$ that previously had to spend half its inputs on $Y$ can now see $\alpha$ bits of $X$ alone, so a chunk grows from 2 bits to $\alpha$ bits and the fast carry logic links the chunks as before. The chunk count, and hence the LUT count *and* the chain depth, both drop by the same factor.

> [!warning] AMD tooling does not do this
> At the time the book was written, AMD tools do not implement the optimization: they fall back on the plain logic optimizer for small $w$ and on a standard (non-specialized) comparator for larger $w$ (p. 149). Getting the constant version means generating it yourself, e.g. `flopoco IntConstantComparator flags=1 w=64 c=17979737894628297144`, which decomposes the 64-bit input into 13 chunks of 5 bits linked by fast carry logic. See also [[Multiplication by Constants]] for the same "constant collapses the operator" pattern.

## Trade-offs

| Variant | LUTs for $X<Y$ | Depth / latency | Page | Stops being the right answer when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subtract and check the sign (generic adder) | $w$ | one $w$-long carry chain | 148 | immediately, unless the difference $X-Y$ is needed anyway — otherwise it pays for $w$ sum bits it throws away |
| Dedicated binary tree (Sect. 6.2) | $w$ XNORs $+$ AND tree for $X{=}Y$; 2 bits/edge otherwise | $\lceil\log_2 w\rceil$ | 146–147 | on FPGA, essentially always: its edges cross general routing, which the dedicated carry path beats for all practical $w$ (p. 149). It is the ASIC/asymptotic answer, and the mental model |
| Fast carry, 2-bit chunks | $\lceil w/2\rceil$; $\lceil w/3\rceil$ for $X{=}Y$ on LUT6 | one chain of $\lceil w/2\rceil$; best latency to 64 bits | 148 | latency-wise past $\approx 64$ bits; area-wise it never does |
| Two-level tree of fast carry | $\approx w/3$ (level 1) $+\ \approx\sqrt{w}/2$ (level 2) | two chains of $\approx\sqrt{w}$ | 149 | below several thousand bits, where its level-1 equality overhead buys latency you did not need |
| LUT-based constant comparison | $\lceil w/\alpha\rceil$; $\alpha=5$ giving 13 LUTs at $w=64$ | one chain of $\lceil w/\alpha\rceil$ | 149 | when $Y$ is not constant, or when no generator is available (AMD tools skip it) |

Pick **fast carry with 2-bit chunks** by default: it is what the tools already do, it is the area optimum, and up to 64 bits it is also the latency optimum. Pick the **two-level tree** only when $w$ reaches the thousands and latency actually binds — its area is strictly worse. Pick the **constant comparator** whenever one operand is fixed (thresholds, exponent bounds, zero-padded fixed-point operands): it is a free $\alpha/2$ reduction in both area and depth, but you must generate it.

> [!warning] No measured numbers in this chapter
> Chapter 6 gives no synthesis table: no ns delays, no slice or FF counts, no device-by-device figures. The quantitative claims above are the LUT-count formulas and the two crossover statements ("best latency up to 64 bits", "several thousand bits") the text actually makes. Anything more precise needs outside research.

## Worked example: 16-bit comparison against a constant

Compare $X$ against the fixed threshold

$$C = \mathtt{0xB3A7} = 1011\,0011\,1010\,0111_2$$

**Variable-operand baseline.** $\lceil 16/2\rceil = 8$ LUTs, 8 carry-chain stages.

**Constant version.** With $Y$ gone, a LUT sees only $X$, so chunks widen to $\alpha$ bits. Both readings of $\alpha$ agree here: the book's $p(a,b,c,d)$/$q(a,b,c,d)$ BLE model gives $\alpha=4$ and $\lceil 16/4\rceil=4$; FloPoCo's 5-bit chunking (13 chunks for $w=64$) gives $\lceil 16/5\rceil=4$. **4 LUTs, 4 carry stages** — half the area and half the depth of the variable comparator.

Take the 5-bit split $C = \underbrace{10110}_{C_3}\ \underbrace{01110}_{C_2}\ \underbrace{10011}_{C_1}\ \underbrace{1}_{C_0}$ and the input $X = \mathtt{0xB3A0} = 1011\,0011\,1010\,0000_2$, i.e.

$$X = \underbrace{10110}_{X_3}\ \underbrace{01110}_{X_2}\ \underbrace{10000}_{X_1}\ \underbrace{0}_{X_0}$$

Each chunk's LUT pair is now a function of 5 input bits only. For chunk 3, $C_3=10110$, the equality bit is a single 5-literal product term

$$E_3 = x_{15}\,\overline{x_{14}}\,x_{13}\,x_{12}\,\overline{x_{11}}$$

— one AND, no XNORs, because each $c_i$ picked the polarity of its literal. The greater-than bit $G_3 = [\,X_3 > 10110\,]$ is likewise a 5-variable function; both fit the LUT pair driving one carry stage.

Chain, LSB first, seeded with $c_0=1$ (the $+1$ of $X+\overline{C}+1$):

| stage | $X_j$ | $C_j$ | verdict | role | $c_{j+1}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | $0$ | $1$ | $<$ | kill | $0$ |
| 1 | $10000$ | $10011$ | $<$ | kill | $0$ |
| 2 | $01110$ | $01110$ | $=$ | propagate | $0$ |
| 3 | $10110$ | $10110$ | $=$ | propagate | $0$ |

$c_4 = 0$, so $X\ge C$ is false: $\mathbf{X<C}$, correct since $\mathtt{0xB3A0} < \mathtt{0xB3A7}$. Note the shape of the prefix reduction — the two top chunks abstain, and the verdict produced at stage 1 propagates untouched to the output. Had $X_3$ been $10111$, stage 3 would have generated and overridden everything below it in one carry step.

Final error: **exactly 0 ulp.** Comparison is a decision, not an approximation; the only failure mode is a wrong format alignment before the compare, which is why the reduction in the first section is stated as an exact, order-preserving rewriting.
