block you are documenting.

Your specific assignment (file name, topic, source pages, sub-blocks) is in the

## Required structure

**1. Specification first** — 3–6 lines before anything else: input format, output
format, accuracy promised (in ulp), and what is being optimized (area / latency /
energy / throughput). Every design decision below must trace back to this. This
is the book's central thesis — "computing just right" — and it is what keeps the
derivations from wandering.

**2. Diagram** — a fenced ` ```tikz ` block showing the datapath or block
structure, with **every signal labelled with its bit width**. The site compiles
these to SVG at build time; use `tikz` for block diagrams, `circuitikz` for
gate/transistor schematics. Give it `title="..."` for alt text. Draw it yourself
from your understanding — do not try to embed the PDF image. Caption it with the
figure it is based on: *after Fig. 8.12, p. 227*.

If a figure is too complex to redraw faithfully (dense FPGA tiling maps,
multi-panel plots), crop it out of the PDF instead — headless, no viewer:

```sh
# locate the caption's y-coordinate, in PDF points
pdftotext -f <pdfpage> -l <pdfpage> -bbox Dienchin.pdf - | grep -i 'Fig\.'
# crop-render the band above it;  pixels = points × (dpi/72)
pdftoppm -f <pdfpage> -l <pdfpage> -r 150 -png -x <X> -y <Y> -W <W> -H <H> \
  Dienchin.pdf assets/<slug>
```

Then `Read` the resulting PNG to confirm you framed it correctly, adjust `-y`/`-H`
and re-render if not. Write it to `Notes/Digital Design/assets/` and reference it
as `![alt](assets/name-01.png)`. Prefer redrawing; crop only when redrawing would
misrepresent the figure. Report every crop in your final message.

**3. Derivation** — the mathematics, in KaTeX (`$...$` inline, `$$...$$` display).
**Name the law at every rewriting step.** Examples of the habit:

- "by distributivity of AND over OR"
- "two's-complement identity $-x = \bar{x} + 1$"
- "associativity lets us reassociate the carry chain into a prefix tree"
- "absorbing the constant column into the bit heap"

A reader must be able to reproduce every line without the book open. Ground all
format and error reasoning in `[[1.1) Number Formats]]` and
`[[1.2) Error Accuracy]]` — link to them rather than re-deriving ulp, rounding
modes, or two's-complement basics.

**4. Sub-blocks** — one `##` section per sub-block named in your assignment. Each
covers: how it works, what it costs, and when it wins over its siblings.

**5. Trade-off table** — the variants side by side with the source's **actual
numbers** (LUT/slice count, logic depth, delay, DSP blocks, cycle count) and the
page each number came from. Close with 2–3 lines of plain "pick X when …, pick Y
when …". A note without this table has not answered "when do I use this".

**6. Worked example** — concrete small operands (8-bit is usually right). Show
every intermediate in binary. State the final error in ulp. Skip only if the
block involves no math worth walking, and say so in your final message.

**7. `## References`** — the sections and page numbers you actually used.

## Style

Read `Notes/System Design/1) The Laplace Domain.md` before you start. Match it:
derivations inline rather than deferred, property tables, `> [!note]` and
`> [!important]` callouts reserved for the one idea that actually matters most in
the note, prose that explains *why* a thing is built the way it is.

Beyond that:

- **FPGA-specific mapping gets its own `##`.** This reader targets both ASIC and
  FPGA, and the book is unusually good on the FPGA side.
- **Wikilinks** use exactly the names in `Notes/Digital Design/Overview.md`.
  Sub-block links take the form `[[2.5) Dividers#SRT Division]]`. Linking to a
  note that does not exist yet is expected and fine.
- Markdown image syntax only — never Obsidian `![[embed]]` syntax.
- No filler, no "in conclusion", no restating the heading in the first sentence.
  Cut any section with nothing real to say.
