Thesis: A typical GEMM / GEMV occurs in two passes, a Prefill stage which is Compute bound, and a Decode stage which is memory-bound. Analog IMC architectures have been proven a faster latency than typical Systollic Arrays, however, they also are more dense toks/s/mm^2 that the systollic array, and more importantly have a higher cieling of compute-bound they can reach at the cost of being //harder to design//.

To start this project, I had to tackle a lot of tooling issues, and also invented new tooling such that I can benefit from the usage of LLMs. I know it sounds like LLMs suck, but genuinely every digital designer I know uses it now because of how scary good it is (Opus 5.5 is at least). Even my old manager, currently at Meta, uses it extensively and just system designs. Why can't we analog designers use it for that exact same reason if it is good. See the Tooling page below, I talk about this and how I made it work. \\

Now, on the bread and butter. The actual Analog IMC that I've opted to call //High-Throughput Analog In-Memory Compute//, mainly because I realized that Analog IMC has very low latency, which could be good for batch, however, done right. It can be done MUCH denser, and provide more compute, and aid the memory-bound issue in systollic arrays, and in-general GEMM operations on chips like Etched's Sohu chip. Hence, my research compares the systollic array measurements I can find online of Sohu's chip and other ones versus mine on ASAP7 (A non-fabricatable PDK but should be good enough).

==== Architecture ====
A streaming, charge-domain IMC die. Weights stream from HBM into analog tiles just ahead of the activations that use them. Each tile multiplies by passive charge sharing and reads out once per weight column. A digital rail (need to covnert to analog) does attention, softmax and the KV cache.

Prefill: All the weights are made capacitances. \\
Multiply is: Q = C * V, Voltage streamed in and produces charge. \\
Accumulate is: The column adding up all the charges
